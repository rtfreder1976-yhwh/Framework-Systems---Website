You don't have a marketing problem.
You have a follow-up problem.

Most plumbing companies don't need more leads. They need to stop losing the ones they already get. Here are the four ways that happens.

The call you never knew about.

You're elbow-deep in a water heater swap. Phone rings. Goes to voicemail. The homeowner doesn't leave a message — 85% of them don't. They call the next plumber. That plumber picks up. Quotes in two hours. Books the job by dinner. You check your phone at 5 PM and see one missed call. No voicemail. So you shrug it off. But that was a $600 job. And it happened three times this week. Do the math on that over a year.

The quote that showed up two days late.

Monday: homeowner requests an estimate for a repipe. You're booked solid. Tuesday: still running calls. Wednesday: you finally sit down and send the quote. Too late. They signed with the company that responded Monday afternoon. Speed to quote is the single biggest predictor of close rate in plumbing. Not price. Not quality. Speed. And you're not even tracking it.

200 jobs this year. 11 reviews.

You replaced the water heater. The homeowner shook your hand. Said "I'll leave you a review." They meant it. They just forgot. Because life happened. And you didn't have a system that reminded them. Now here's what stings: the plumber across town — the one you know does mediocre work — has 140 Google reviews. You have 11. Guess who gets the next call?

Your crew drove 30 minutes to an empty house.

The homeowner booked a diagnostic. No confirmation text went out. No reminder the day before. They forgot. Your crew shows up. Nobody home. That's an hour of windshield time, an hour of labor, and a slot you could have filled with a paying job — gone. Now multiply that by three or four times a month. That's real money.
